Technology, Ethics, & Society

If computer-mediated communications change how we interact with each other, then different groups competing to shape emerging communications technologies may also be vying over the future of human society.

In order to address contemporary issues, it will be necessary to think about the design of new technologies in a different way: not from the limited perspective of marketing and design, but from an interdisciplinary perspective that focuses on the impact of various design choices and integrates insights from social science and philosophy.

Technology and Society

Humans have an obligation to think about the potential consequences of their actions, a point articulated in both philosophy and law. We need to think before we act. Reflection upon history and technology, philosophy and ethics, social engineering and design provides a context for looking at the world we live in. It helps us understand the world and make intelligent choices. Or, to put it a bit differently, it helps us make important policy and design decisions. It is in the context of these decisions that the study of Technology and Society and the Ethics of Technology becomes meaningful.

There is a rich and diverse body of work documenting the impact of new communications technologies and of various design choices. The field of Technology and Society, including anthropology and history, gives insight into contemporary and future issues and problems: What issues are likely to arise? How may they be understood? How may they be addressed?

In 1905, the journal Telephony reflected:

The invention of new machinery, devices, and processes is continually bringing up new questions of law, puzzling judges, lawyers, and laymen.

With the introduction of a new communications technology, whether we are talking about the telegraph, the telephone, or the Internet, comes a period of initial confusion in which social norms and practices are renegotiated as different groups negotiate how a technology is used, designed, and understood. This is what has happened on the Internet with issues such as digital rights management, open-source software, identity theft, and privacy.

Past technologies, such as the telegraph, may give us a great deal of insight into contemporary problems. This point was popularized by Tom Standage, who explains that the telegraph, the “highway of thought,” was a worldwide communications network whose cables spanned continents and oceans. It revolutionized business practices and was believed to promote world peace and harmony.

On the telegraph there were budding romances, online weddings, new forms of crime, failed government regulations, and concerns about information overload and security. There is a large body of research documenting the parallels between previous communications technologies, such as the telegraph and telephone, and contemporary Internet technologies. In this way, the history of technology gives us insight into the problems that we face today.

Communication and Design

The field of Communication looks more specifically at the impact of various design choices and how they influence interpersonal communication. Scholars explain that in the design of virtual workgroups and virtual communities, for instance, it is possible to influence group dynamics and promote teamwork, creativity, or productivity.

Nancy Baym, for example, looks at how online group interaction emerges out of complex interactions between design variables. Thorngren explains why long-distance relationships over the telephone are difficult to maintain over time unless reinforced by face-to-face interaction or social, family, or business ties. Nicholas Yee examines how conditioning may be used to shape behavior in massively multiplayer online games and how the structuring of activity may be used to promote teamwork and bonding.

Recommended Reading

  • Nicholas Yee
  • “The Emergence of On-Line Community,” Nancy Baym

Additional Reading

  • Power, Madness, and Immortality: The Future of Virtual Reality , Mychilo Cline
  • Communication in the Age of Virtual Reality, edited by Frank Biocca and Mark R. Levy
  • “Silent Actors: Communication Networks for Development,” Bent Thorngren, in The Social Impact of the Telephone
  • “A Group Is Its Own Worst Enemy,” “Economics and Culture,” “Media and Community,” and “Open Source,” Clay Shirky

Political Philosophy, Ethics, and Science Fiction

Political philosophy and ethics also give us insight into the design of Internet and virtual reality technologies. The development of these technologies raises traditional moral questions about their use, impact, and design; existential questions about who we want to be; and questions about the relationship between the individual and the state.

The history of Western philosophy provides insight and perspective into these questions and grounds the discussion. In the words of Carolyn Marvin:

. . . patterns anchored in older media that have provided the stable currency of social exchange are re-examined, challenged, and defended.

. . . less the evolution of technical efficiencies in communication than a series of arenas for negotiating issues critical to the conduct of social life.

Science fiction provides an alternate entry point for the discussion of political philosophy and ethics. Science fiction provides insight into possible futures, problems created by new technologies, and questions about the relationship between the individual and the state.

In addition, books such as William Gibson’s Neuromancer and Neal Stephenson’s Snow Crash, described by Bruce Damer as the Old and New Testaments within the development community, have helped guide, shape, and inspire the development of new technologies.

Recommended Reading

  • 1984, George Orwell
  • Brave New World, Aldous Huxley
  • Neuromancer, William Gibson
  • Cryptonomicon, Neal Stephenson
  • The Diamond Age, Neal Stephenson

A Growing Area of Concern

More recently, there has been growing interest in the impact of technology upon society, as illustrated by increased attention within academia, government institutions, the development community, and the general public. The rapid development of artificial intelligence makes these questions even more immediate, because decisions about how AI systems are designed and used may shape work, education, communication, creativity, and social power.
