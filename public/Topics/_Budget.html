REAL LIFE BUDGET

How far does a $120,000 household income really go?

You are a two-adult household earning $120,000 per year. Your goal is to build the life you want, pay for it, and see what is left.

START WITH YOUR YEARLY INCOME

  • Gross household income: $120,000
  • 401(k): 10% of income = $12,000 per year
  • Taxes: about $17,800 per year (federal income tax + Social Security + Medicare)
  • Health insurance: $500 per month = $6,000 per year

Start with $120,000. Subtract $12,000 for the 401(k), about $17,800 in taxes, and $6,000 for health insurance. That leaves about $84,200 per year to build your budget.

1. CHOOSE YOUR HOUSING

  1. Find a two-bedroom apartment in the Aubrey/Frisco area that you would actually live in.
  2. Write down the monthly rent.
  3. Add $400 per month for electricity, water, trash, internet, renter's insurance, and phone.
  4. Find the total monthly housing cost, then multiply by 12.

Subtract the YEARLY housing cost from your remaining yearly income.

2. CHOOSE YOUR CAR

  1. Find a car you would actually buy and determine its monthly payment.
  2. Add $500 per month for the rest of the car: $200 insurance + $200 gas + $100 routine maintenance/registration.
  3. Find the total monthly car cost, then multiply by 12.

Subtract the YEARLY car cost.

3. FOOD

  • Groceries for two adults: $800 per month.
  • Multiply by 12 and subtract the yearly cost.
  • Eating out, coffee, dates, etc. do NOT come from groceries. Those come from your fun money.

4. FUN MONEY

  1. Choose how much money you want available each WEEK for restaurants, coffee, movies, dates, entertainment, and other fun.
  2. Convert your weekly amount to a yearly amount: weekly fun money × 52.
  3. Subtract it from your yearly income.

5. YEARLY CHOICES

These are not monthly bills. Decide what kind of life you want and choose a YEARLY amount for each.

  • Travel / vacation
  • Hobbies
  • Clothes / makeup / beauty / spa / sports / other personal spending
  • Christmas / holiday gifts

Add these yearly choices together and subtract the total.

6. WHAT IS LEFT?

After every deduction above, calculate how much of your original $120,000 is still unspent.

  • Money left for the year: __________________
  • Money left per month (yearly amount ÷ 12): __________________

THEN LIFE HAPPENS

A balanced budget does not guarantee that life follows the budget. Be ready for expenses such as:

  • Car repair
  • Unexpected medical or dental bill
  • Other major unexpected expense
  • Have a child: add about $12,000/year for childcare + $2,000/year for other child expenses (simplified class estimate).

LITTLE EXPENSES ADD UP

  • $7 coffee, 5 days a week = $1,820 per year
  • $10 spent every day = $3,650 per year
  • One pack of cigarettes a day: calculate the yearly cost.
  • Choose one of your own habits or small purchases and calculate its yearly cost.

FINAL QUESTION: Does the life you chose fit the income you have?
