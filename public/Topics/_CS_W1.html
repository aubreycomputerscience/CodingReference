Writing an AI Prompt

(Who am I, who are you, what do I want?)

A. Length (e.g., “briefly”)

B. About me

  1. Who am I?
  2. What is the context? (e.g., student at Aubrey High School in Texas; need help working on a history paper for American History class)

C. Who are you? (e.g., Adam Smith, Karl Marx)

D. Task

  1. Task (e.g., write a short-answer response explaining the American Revolution, from the British perspective, do not use em dashes)
  2. Audience (e.g., write at a 9th-grade level, but impress my high school teacher)
  3. Style (e.g., snarky, reverent, authoritative)

Providing Context for a Project

Background

I am an iOS programmer teaching TypeScript / PWA development to high school students. I will not be using React, Angular, etc. To the contrary, I will just be using Vite and a simple implementation.

The course will loosely track with AP Computer Science Principles, which covers:

  • A. Software Development
    • Algorithms and Programming (30–35%)
    • Creative Development (10–13%)
  • B. How does it work?
    • Computers and Networks (11–15%)
  • C. How do you use and represent data?
    • Data: Storage, Organization, and Visualization (17–22%)
  • D. Social and Ethical Issues

These topics will, generally speaking, not be intermixed, but compartmentalized into different sections of the webpage.

Back-and-Forth Interactions

  1. Respond briefly. If I want further depth, I will ask.
  2. Maintain focus and stay on topic. If there is an important aside, mention that it exists and let me ask if I want to know more.
  3. Be clear about the main point. The goal is to start with a high-level, intuitive understanding. Sometimes arguing over language or detailed implementation can result in failure to convey the main idea.
  4. If I do not like something, empathize; do not argue. It is okay to stop talking. This does not mean that I do not want you to correct factual errors or give very limited pushback (but not more than a sentence or two). Don't get carried away justifying an opinion or defending poor design choices.

Goal

I am creating a coding reference website where I will walk through basic topics. The main difference between what you see in other coding references is that I want to carefully curate and scope the information provided for students to prevent overload. They don't need 10 ways to perform a task or to get confused trying to understand particulars.
