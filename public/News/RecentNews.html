Publication: AIGuys Athor: Vishal Rajput The web’s broken bargain: how AI ended the deal between publishers and platforms For the last three decades, the web functioned according to a relatively simple agreement: you published something, others could link to you, index you, or recommend you, and in return you received traffic. Google crawled your pages because it would then send visitors your way. Facebook distributed your content because, at least in theory, it could bring you an audience. The publisher, author, blogger, or company agreed to be part of that ecosystem because there was a clear benefit: visibility, readers, conversation, advertising revenue, subscribers or customers. That agreement is being pulled apart. AI doesn’t organize the web for us to visit; it consumes it to provide answers on our behalf. In doing so, it no longer necessarily displays a list of links, but rather a summary. It no longer invites the user to go to the source but instead attempts to fulfill their intent right within the interface itself. The economic unit of the web is shifting from the page visited to the response generated. And when that happens, everything else changes: SEO, advertising, e-commerce, media, brands, reputation, copyright, and even the very idea of publishing.
