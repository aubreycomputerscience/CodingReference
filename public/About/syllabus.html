Course Syllabus

Welcome

Welcome to Computer Science. The goal of these courses is to teach students to build real applications using real development tools while developing the problem-solving skills needed to understand and create computer technologies. Rather than simply working through a textbook, students will learn computer science by building lightweight apps, games, and other software projects.

App development will serve as the entry point into computer science. Through this work, students will learn programming fundamentals, development tools, data structures, algorithms, software design, and how computer systems operate. We will also examine the social and ethical effects of computer technologies.

The course is designed to remove much of the unnecessary friction that can make learning to program difficult. At the same time, programming requires patience, persistence, and a willingness to revise work when something does not function as expected. Students will be expected to think carefully, solve complex problems, and take responsibility for their progress.

Computer Science I introduces programming, software development, and foundational computer science concepts. Computer Science II builds on those foundations through more complex programs, application design, data structures, testing, and software-development practices. Computer Science III continues the development of programming and software-engineering skills through advanced and extended coursework. Instruction and project difficulty may be adjusted to reflect the course level, students’ prior preparation, and demonstrated needs.

How the Class Works

New programming concepts will often be introduced through guided coding activities in which students follow along with the teacher. Students will then apply those concepts through independent practice. The course will also include more traditional instruction, demonstrations, discussion, and written problem-solving.

As the year progresses, students will work on increasingly substantial projects and will be expected to plan, develop, test, and revise their work with greater independence.

Grading

Grades will be based on projects, project check-ins, quizzes, exams, and other assigned work. Major grades count for 60% of the course grade, while minor and check-in grades count for 40%.

Late Work and Make-Up Work

This course follows Aubrey ISD grading policies. Assignments submitted one day late receive a 10-point deduction, assignments submitted two days late receive a 20-point deduction, and assignments submitted three or more days late receive a 30-point deduction. Late work must be submitted within the applicable grading period.

Work missed because of an absence follows the district’s make-up-work policy and may be completed within the allowed make-up period without a late penalty. Students who fail a major assessment will also have an opportunity to complete required reteaching and attempt the assessment again, with the replacement grade capped at 70.

Technology and Acceptable Use

Students must follow Aubrey ISD’s Acceptable Use Policy in this class and throughout the school day. Misuse of a school computer in any class may result in the loss of computer privileges. A student who loses those privileges can still complete the course, but coding assignments may have to be completed using pen and paper, which is substantially more difficult.

Students in these courses may receive additional computer permissions needed to develop and test software. These privileges carry additional responsibility. Serious or repeated violations of district technology policies will be addressed firmly and may result in the immediate loss of those privileges, along with other consequences required by school or district policy.

Student Expectations

Students are expected to arrive ready to learn, participate actively, use class time productively, and treat others with respect. They should ask questions when they need help and remain willing to revise their work.

Programming involves mistakes, uncertainty, and problems that may not have an immediate solution. Persistence is therefore an essential part of success in this course.

Artificial intelligence tools, including MagicSchool, may be used only under conditions explicitly established by the teacher to support student learning. Students may not use AI or other outside resources to complete work on their behalf. Submitted work must reflect the student’s own understanding, and students may be asked to explain or revise their work.

Materials

Students should bring a notebook and pencil to class each day. Although this is a computer science course, a substantial amount of planning, problem-solving, tracing, design, assessment, and discussion will be completed offline.

Communication

Assignments, announcements, and grades will be posted through the school’s online systems. The best way to contact me is by school email. I will respond within a reasonable time during the school week.
